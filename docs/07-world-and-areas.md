# 07 — World & Areas

## Geography

**Limpo Kingdom** — rural, poor, warm. Two centuries of economic decline driven by on-and-off
conflict with Yettallia. Villages and one capital. Farmland that isn't producing, memorials in every
town square, a generation of missing people nobody talks about directly.

**Yettallia** — hyper-industrial, wealthy, loud. Neon and smoke. A war economy that won. It is the
better place to live and the worse place to be.

**Vixtry Co.** sits across both and belongs to neither, which is the first thing suspicious about
it. Its billboards in Limpo promise escape from hardship. Its billboards in Yettallia promise
convenience. Same product.

Neither country is the real setting. Both are running on hardware in a building the player never
sees.

Five hubs, five liminal dungeons, alternating. Each contains exactly one hidden collectible
([09](09-collectibles-and-endings.md)).

### 1. The Gallery — *Act 0.5*

The brick building from Images 1 and 4, standing in absolute black with no approach path. Inside:
grass floor, brick walls, drop-tile fluorescent ceiling, and five framed paintings of stone
primitives in still water (Image 3). Hallways loop and run longer than the exterior allows.

No enemies. No music, just a fluorescent hum. It's a tutorial for movement and interaction and
nothing else, and it should take about fifteen minutes, most of which the player spends deciding
whether something is about to happen.

**Revisitable** from Act 4 onward. It has changed.

### 2. Okobo Village — *Act 1 hub*

A few hundred residents. Crooked buildings in saturated pinks and yellows, a well, a shop, an inn, a
war memorial nobody stands near. The residents are strange-looking, warm, chatty, and share a verbal
tic ([10](10-writing-guide.md)).

**Contains:** first shop, first inn/save point, the game's gentlest sidequests, and the **first
Custodian sighting** (~30 min in, non-interactive).

The Okobo theme is the prettiest music in the game. This is on purpose.

### 3. The Sunken Orchard — *Act 1 liminal zone*

A fruit orchard under ankle-deep still water that extends past the horizon in every direction. Every
tree is the same tree — the same model, the same rotation, the same three fruit. Reflections in the
water are correct for the sky but wrong for the trees.

Low encounter density. The sound is footsteps in water and nothing else. **Mini-Boss 1** at the
centre, in a clearing where the water is deeper.

### 4. Ondo — *Act 2 hub, capital of Limpo*

The largest Limpo settlement and the game's sidequest engine. Market district, boarding house,
government block, a park with a dry fountain. Visibly a capital that used to be grander.

**Contains:** the best mid-game shop, ~8 sidequests (the primary money source), the game's **first
Vixtry billboard**, and an optional boarding-house NPC who mentions a man who "went inside and got
better" and is gone afterward.

This is the warmest, busiest, most EarthBound stretch of the game, sitting immediately before
Kestrel Works. That adjacency is the whole point.

### 5. Kestrel Works — *Act 2 liminal zone*

An abandoned multi-floor factory in a region of permanent Siberia-grade winter. Reachable only by
following a rumour or reading an unmarked road sign — a player who doesn't read things has to be
told where it is by an NPC, which is a small, deliberate embarrassment.

Cold, dark, mechanically dead. The lore here is a decade of layoff notices found in stages, floor by
floor, ending with a note from the last person to leave. **Second Custodian sighting**, standing in
a cleared room on the way back out.

**Main Boss 1** in the factory yard, beneath a war memorial. (Obelisk — see [08](08-bosses.md).)

### 6. Sable City — *Act 3 hub, Yettallia*

Neon, smoke, vertical. Elevated rail, storefronts stacked six high, crowds that don't stop. The
first place in the game that is louder than the player.

**Contains:** the best shop tier before the endgame, a transit hub with a **Vixtry recruitment
desk**, storefront demo pods where citizens are already using the product, and the first fights
against Vixtry subordinates.

**Mini-Boss 2** in the industrial district.

### 7. Bellhouse Commons — *Act 3 liminal zone*

An enormous apartment complex, mostly empty. The interior is non-Euclidean: corridors longer than
the building, stairwells that arrive on the floor they left, rooms that connect where they can't.
Wall murals throughout are trippy and elaborate and match nothing about the exterior.

Roughly four apartments are occupied. The tenants are polite, home, and completely uninterested in
him. One is watching television with the screen facing the wall.

**Third Custodian sighting** — first time he speaks. `Never leave.` Cannot be attacked.
**First warp device** is found here. **Main Boss 2** at the top. (Cube.)

### 8. Vixtry Regional Campus — *Act 4*

A corporate campus rendered as liminal space *without being abandoned.* Fully staffed, brightly lit,
clean. Nobody stops the player. Nobody asks what he's doing. **Employees greet him by name**, which
he never gave them, and the game does not comment on this.

Functions as a hostile hub: there's a vending machine that works as a shop, benches that work as
save points, and enemies that are also receptionists.

**Contains:** the game's densest lore concentration (internal memos), the confirmation of what
Vixtry actually does, and **Mini-Boss 3**.

### 9. The Long Hall — *Act 4 liminal zone*

The Gallery's hallway, reskinned and much longer. Same brick, same drop-tile ceiling, same grass.
The paintings are gone; the frames are not. Walking it takes a genuinely uncomfortable amount of
time and there is nothing in it except one collectible and the door at the end.

**Main Boss 3** at the end: the Custodian, first form. (Sphere.)

### 10. The Root — *Act 5*

The final area. What the dream world looks like when it stops maintaining itself: Okobo's geometry
with Kestrel's lighting, Bellhouse's corridors opening onto the Sunken Orchard, the bedroom's
furniture in the middle of a field. Colour drained nearly to the real-world palette. Grass on
everything.

Enemies here are recognizable — earlier species, wrong colours, wrong levels.

**Contains:** **Mini-Boss 4**, the last collectible, and **Main Boss 4** — the Custodian's true
form. (Pyramid.)

## Area design rules

1. **Every hub has:** a shop, a save point, an inn or equivalent full-restore, at least three
   sidequests, and one NPC whose dialogue changes after the next major story beat.
2. **Every liminal zone has:** one collectible, at least three readable notes, at most one boss,
   near-silent audio, and no shop.
3. **Encounter density is inverse to atmosphere.** If a room is meant to be unsettling, it has no
   enemies in it.
4. **No area is permanently missable.** Every location can be revisited after the warp network comes
   online, including the Gallery.
5. **Saturation falls as depth increases.** The last room of every dungeon should be lit like the
   bedroom in Act 0.
