# 09 — Collectibles & Endings

## The ten hidden items

Ten collectible Special Items, **one per major area** ([07](07-world-and-areas.md)). Finding all ten
unlocks the secret ending.

### Placement rules

- **Never on the critical path.** A player following the story will not trip over one.
- **Never signposted by text.** No NPC mentions them. No note references them. There is no in-game
  checklist until the player has found the first one, at which point a bag category appears showing
  `1/10` and nothing else — no names, no locations, no hints.
- **Hidden in the geometry, not behind a puzzle.** Behind unremarkable scenery, in an unmarked
  side-room, down a corridor the camera discourages, inside an object nobody else in the game is
  interactive.
- **At least three require late-game tools or access** to reach in early-game areas — soft
  Metroidvania backtracking, made practical by the warp network coming online mid-game
  ([06](06-items-and-equipment.md)). The Gallery collectible is one of these: the Gallery is only
  revisitable from Act 4.
- **The overgrowth is the tell.** Per [01](01-art-and-audio.md), grass density rises subtly near
  something true. A collectible room has visibly more green than the rooms around it. This is never
  stated and is the only assistance the game offers.

### What they are

Ten items that individually look like junk and collectively don't. Recommended direction: **objects
from the boy's real bedroom**, rendered as dream-world detritus — a marble, a torn poster corner, a
loose key, half a photograph. Each has flavour text of one short sentence that describes the object
neutrally and never explains it.

The player who finds three will think they're set dressing. The player who finds ten will have
assembled the contents of a room they've already seen, in Act 0, in the dark.

| # | Area | Placement direction |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Gallery | Behind one of the five paintings. Requires Act 4 revisit. |
| 2 | Okobo Village | Down the village well, reachable only with a later traversal item. |
| 3 | The Sunken Orchard | Under the water in the deep clearing, visible only after Mini-Boss 1. |
| 4 | Ondo | In a municipal building's records room, in a drawer nobody else opens. |
| 5 | Kestrel Works | In a locker on a floor the critical path skips. |
| 6 | Sable City | Behind a Vixtry billboard, on a maintenance catwalk. |
| 7 | Bellhouse Commons | In an occupied apartment, in plain sight, while the tenant doesn't react. |
| 8 | Vixtry Campus | Misfiled in a filing system — the player has to read labels to find the wrong one. |
| 9 | The Long Hall | In the grass, roughly two-thirds down the corridor, off the walking line. |
| 10 | The Root | Somewhere the player has already walked past nine times. |

## Standard ending

Available to any player who beats Main Boss 4.

He wakes. Genuinely. The final scene mirrors the opening bedroom shot exactly — same camera, same
room, same five interactable objects — with **one thing different in the framing**. A light on, a
door open, or someone sitting on the end of the bed.

Nothing narrates the change. No epilogue text crawl. No explanation of what Vixtry was, what
happened to the father, or whether any of it "counted." The player either notices the difference or
doesn't.

Credits.

## Secret ending

Unlocked by holding all ten collectibles when entering the Root.

### What changes

The Root contains one additional door, in a room the player has already cleared, marked only by the
grass being thicker in front of it. Through it: **the Gallery again**, restored, with the five
paintings hung — and the **spire** painting, the odd small one, now the only frame that is empty.

Past that, the **secret encounter** (*Spire*): the Custodian in a form that is smaller than his
first, not larger. He does not attack. The fight has one mechanic and the player will work out what
it is.

### What it reveals

The secret ending narrows the Custodian from three possible readings to two ([03](03-characters.md))
and makes explicit what Vixtry did to the boy specifically — not the company's general ambitions,
which the main game already covers, but the particulars of his case. Who signed. What was measured.
How long he'd been in.

**It does not answer whether his father knew.** Nothing in the game does. That stays open in both
endings, and the temptation to close it in the secret ending should be resisted — the secret ending
rewards attention with *detail*, not with resolution.

### The final shot

Same bedroom. Same camera. This time **two** things are different.

---

## Design intent

The two endings are not "bad" and "good." The standard ending is complete, satisfying, and coherent
on its own — a player who never finds a single collectible has finished the whole game and been told
a whole story.

The secret ending is for the player who was paying the kind of attention the protagonist wished
someone would pay to him. That's the actual reward, and it's why the collectibles are objects from
his room rather than power-ups.
