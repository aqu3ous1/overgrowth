# 10 — Writing Guide

Rules and voice references for anyone writing dialogue in Overgrowth. Sample lines are direction,
not final script.

## Global rules

1. **The protagonist never speaks.** Not in dialogue, not in battle text, not in item flavour. The
   battle log reads `PUNCH!`, never `You punch.`
2. **NPCs use contractions constantly. Menus and system text never do.** This keeps the system voice
   subtly inhuman without anyone consciously registering why. Vixtry employees use the system voice.
3. **Four lines maximum per text box.** Including the Custodian. Including the finale.
4. **No line explains a theme.** If a draft contains the words "alone," "lonely," "family," or
   "real" in a summarizing way, cut it. The systems carry the theme; the dialogue carries the
   world.
5. **No winking.** No character is aware they're in a game, a simulation, or a horror story — with
   exactly one exception, at the very end, and even then only obliquely.
6. **The family's names are never revealed.** Not the surname, not the father's name, not the
   mother's. Parents are referred to by role only — "Mum," "Dad," "your father," "one of the
   hardware guys" — and no NPC ever addresses a parent directly, because an NPC who did would have
   to use a name. The protagonist knows all three; the player never finds out. Full rule and the
   three places it's dodged: [15](15-lore-notes.md).

## Voice by faction

### Okobo and Limpo villagers

Warm, chatty, digressive. Odd turns of phrase and small comic asides. Strong opinions about local
produce. Harmless superstitions delivered with total confidence.

They share a **verbal tic** that marks them as not-quite-Earth without making them alien.
**Recommendation:** they attach a redundant location to statements about time — "back-when, over in
the spring" / "later-on, up in the evening." It reads as an accent, it's easy to write
consistently, and it makes the reader's ear itch slightly without ever being explained.

They are also poor, and quietly grieving a two-century war. Both facts appear in passing, never as
a speech.

> "Oh — you're the one who came down the hill. Everyone said someone came down the hill."

> "We don't do much with the north field, back-when. Nothing wrong with it. Just nobody left to do
> much with it."

> "My brother went off. Later-on, up in the spring. That was some springs ago now."

### Yettallia citizens

Busier, blunter, transactional. Not unkind — running late. Where a Limpo villager gives three
paragraphs about a nephew, a Yettallia citizen gives one sentence and leaves.

No verbal tic. Their marker is brevity.

> "Sure. Second level, past the noodle place. Can't miss it, everyone does."

> "It's fine here. Loud. It's fine."

### Family, friends, and their dream-world mirrors

**Polite, distracted, technically responsive, never present.** They answer questions. They ask none.
They are in the middle of something.

**The test:** if a line would sting to hear, rewrite it. If it would be forgotten by the person who
said it, it's correct. Indifference reads lonelier than cruelty and ages better.

> "Mm. That's good. Hang on — I have to take this."

> "Oh, you're still up? Okay. Well — goodnight, then."

> "We should do something. Sometime. Definitely."

Never let one of these characters be caught out, apologize, or have a moment of realization. That
would be a kind of attention, and they don't pay it.

### Vixtry Co.

Corporate-pleasant at every tier, discussing anything. Never let the writing wink — the horror is
that they mean it sincerely, and their internal logic is coherent enough to follow.

**Grunts** (Act 3) — cheerful, scripted, know nothing:

> "Welcome to co-living! Are you here about the trial, or just curious?"

> "I'm honestly not sure what the back-end does. I'm on retention."

**Field staff** (Act 3–4) — uneasy, deflecting:

> "That's above my level. But I'd say — if it were a problem, we'd have heard."

> "My dad's on the hardware side. He likes it. He says he likes it."

**Officers** (Act 4) — total knowledge, total calm:

> "Dependency isn't the failure case. It's the retention curve working."

> "You've been very consistent. That's rare. Most subjects plateau."

> "I'm sorry. That isn't a figure of speech — I am sorry. It's still going to happen."

**The father reveal** is delivered by a grunt who doesn't know it's significant, flatly, and the
game moves on immediately:

> "Oh — same last name as one of the hardware guys. Huh."

### The Custodian

**Hard rules, repeated from [03](03-characters.md):**

- One line — `Never leave.` — until the true final confrontation.
- The box degrades: kerning collapses, the line repeats to fill all four lines, the box overflows
  its border (Image 2).
- **No advance prompt.** The player does not control when he's done.
- One short additional passage at the climax. Under four lines. It states what he wants. It does not
  explain why he wants it.

If a draft of the climax passage explains his motives, it is wrong — cut it back until it only
states the want. The whole character is built on restraint, and the pressure to give him a monologue
will be enormous.

## Environmental text

### Readable notes

The lore backbone, and always optional ([06](06-items-and-equipment.md)). Written as real documents
by people who had no idea anyone would read them later: layoff notices, a tenant's letter, internal
memos, a shift schedule with one name crossed out.

**Rule:** a note is never *about* the plot. It's about a person's Tuesday. The plot is what the
player assembles from thirty of those.

> **KESTREL WORKS — NOTICE**
> Effective the 14th, second shift is suspended. Affected staff should collect final pay from
> the yard office. We thank you for eleven years.

> Marcy — I'm leaving the key with the super. I don't think I'm coming back for the rest of it.
> Sorry about the wall. It was like that.

### Signage

Vixtry signage is the only non-pixel typography in the game — clean modern sans, pasted on top of
reality:

> **VIXTRY CO.** — *online co-living*
> *stay in. stay together.*

> *Ask about family plans.*

### System text

Cold, monospace, no contractions, no personality:

> `THE ENEMY IS LEVEL 27. YOU CANNOT RUN.`

> `YOU LEARNED WAKE UP.`
