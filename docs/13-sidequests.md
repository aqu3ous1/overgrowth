# 13 — Sidequests

Twenty total, eight of them in Ondo. The primary money source (~45% of player income) and the
game's main opportunity to be charming before it stops being charming.

## Rules

1. **Nothing is failable or timed.** No sidequest can be locked out by story progress. Every one
   remains completable after the warp network comes online.
2. **Every giver has a name and one thing about them that isn't the quest.** A player should be able
   to recall the baker's opinion about his neighbour long after forgetting what the baker wanted.
3. **Rewards never exceed the same act's shop.** Sidequests pay in money, consumables, and
   occasionally a sidegrade — never a power spike. The reward for doing them is being able to afford
   things, not being stronger than the curve.
4. **About a quarter end with the giver slightly less interested than when they started.** They
   thank him. They move on. Nobody who sends the boy on an errand asks him a question about himself.
   No single instance registers; twenty of them accumulate.
5. **The mysteries have boring answers.** When a sidequest sets up something strange, it resolves
   mundanely. The game has exactly one real mystery and it is not on a quest board.

## Okobo Village — Act 1

| # | Quest | Giver | Summary | Reward |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Well Bucket** | Auntie Poll | The bucket's rope broke. Fetch it. It is at the bottom of the well, obviously. | 120 Rell, Spray ×2 |
| 2 | **Someone's Brother** | Hess | Deliver a letter to an address on the north road. The address is a foundation with nothing on it. Hess already knows. He asks anyway. | 200 Rell |
| 3 | **Melon Opinion** | Two neighbours | Settle a dispute about whose melons are better by eating one of each. Either answer ends it. Both parties are satisfied. | Sunned Melon ×3, 80 Rell |

**#2 is the tone-setter for the whole system.** It's an errand, it's polite, it takes four minutes,
and it's the saddest thing in Act 1. Nobody in the game ever mentions it again.

## Ondo — Act 2

The money engine. Eight quests, all available on arrival, all completable in any order.

| # | Quest | Giver | Summary | Reward |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | **The Dry Fountain** | Parks clerk | The fountain hasn't run in years. Find out why. The valve is closed. Someone closed it during rationing and never opened it. Nobody is in charge of opening it. | 350 Rell |
| 6 | **Boarding House Ledger** | Mrs. Ilm | Collect three overdue rents. Two pay. One has left. | 400 Rell, Chalk Tablet ×2 |
| 7 | **Lost Dog (Again)** | Denn | Find the dog. It is at home. It has been at home the whole time. | 200 Rell |
| 8 | **Delivery: Northside** | Courier office | Three parcels, three addresses, no fighting. Pure legwork, and the fastest money in the act. | 500 Rell |
| 9 | **The Bakery Grievance** | Ovel the baker | Ovel is furious with the grocer next door. Deliver a strongly worded note. The grocer laughs and sends a note back. This repeats twice more and then they get lunch together. | 300 Rell, Spray II ×2 |
| 10 | **Records Request** | Municipal clerk | Retrieve a file from the records room. The room is enormous and the file is misfiled. *(The area's hidden collectible is also in here, elsewhere, and the game does not connect the two.)* | 450 Rell |
| 11 | **Park Bench** | Old man on a bench | He wants company for a while. That's the whole quest — sit down and let him talk for six text boxes about a war he was too young for. | 250 Rell, Bitter Tonic ×2 |
| 12 | **A Small Mystery** | Neighbourhood watch | Someone has been moving things in the market at night. It's a bird. It is genuinely just a bird. | 400 Rell, Loose Laces |

**#11 is the only sidequest in the game where an NPC wants something from the boy other than
labour**, and what he wants is for the boy to listen. He does not ask a single question back.

## Kestrel Works — Act 2

| # | Quest | Giver | Summary | Reward |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | **Last One Out** | *(no giver)* | A note on the factory door asks whoever finds it to shut off the yard lights. Doing so is optional, unrewarded by any NPC, and changes the area's lighting permanently. | Overgrowth density increases in the yard |

**No giver, no money, no acknowledgement.** The only reward is that the place gets darker and the
grass gets thicker — which, per [09](09-collectibles-and-endings.md), is the game's only tell for
hidden collectibles. This is the quest that teaches attentive players what the grass means, and it
teaches it by paying them in grass.

## Distribution note

Quests are front-loaded on purpose: 13 of 20 are available before the end of Act 2, and there are
none at all in Acts 5 or in the Long Hall. Late-game money gets tight because the late game has no
one left in it who needs anything from him — which is the economy doing thematic work rather than
just gating purchases ([11](11-pacing-and-systems.md)).
