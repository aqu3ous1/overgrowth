# 14 — Script Samples

Six scenes written out in full, as reference for tone, pacing and box length. Not final script —
these exist so that anyone writing a new scene has something to match.

`[NAME]` is the player-entered name. Stage directions are in italics. Four lines per box, always.

---

## 1. Act 0 — The Bedroom

*Fade in from black to nearly black. The room is a silhouette. A strip of light under the closed
door. Muffled voices through it. No music. Furnace hum, and a clock.*

*The player has control. Five interactables.*

**The window:**
> It's dark out. The neighbours' lights are off.

**The poster:**
> He's had it a long time.

**The dresser:**
> The second drawer sticks.

**The light switch:**
> Nothing happens.
> The hall light is the only one on.

**The door:**
> ▓▓▓▓ ▓▓ ▓▓▓ ▓▓▓▓▓▓ ▓▓.
> ▓▓ ▓▓▓▓▓ ▓▓▓▓▓ ▓▓▓ ▓▓▓▓.
>
> They're arguing again.

*Interacting with the door a second time:*

> ▓▓▓ ▓▓▓▓▓▓ ▓▓▓▓. ▓▓ ▓▓▓▓.
>
> They're arguing again.

*And a third, and a fourth, and every time after. The blocks are regenerated per interaction; the
line underneath never changes.*

*After roughly ninety seconds, or immediately if the player goes to the bed: a door slams,
off-screen. The strip of light under the door goes out. The room goes to full black.*

*Only the bed remains interactable.*

> He goes to bed.

*Hold on black for four seconds longer than is comfortable.*

**Notes.** No one says his name in this scene. No one says anything in this scene. The furnace and
the clock are the only sound design, and the clock is the only thing in the room that responds to
him — it keeps going.

## 2. Act 0.5 — The Gallery, first hallway

*Grass floor. Brick walls. Drop-tile ceiling, fluorescent, faint irregular flicker. Five framed
paintings of stone primitives in still water.*

*No music. Fluorescent hum.*

**The obelisk painting:**
> A tall stone, standing in water.

**The sphere painting:**
> A round stone, standing in water.

**The pyramid painting:**
> A pointed stone, standing in water.

**The cube painting:**
> A square stone, standing in water.

**The spire painting** *(smaller frame, set apart):*
> A tall stone, standing in water.
> It is in a smaller frame than the others.

**The grass:**
> Grass.

**The ceiling:**
> Office ceiling.

**Notes.** The descriptions are near-identical on purpose — a player skimming will read four boxes
that say the same thing and stop reading, which is exactly what should happen on a first
playthrough. The spire's second line is the only variation in the room, and it is a statement about
framing rather than about the painting.

Nothing in the Gallery has a joke in it. It is the only area in the game that doesn't.

## 3. Act 1 — Arriving in Okobo

*Saturated afternoon. Crooked buildings in pink and mustard. Music: warm, detuned, the prettiest
track in the game.*

**First villager the player speaks to:**
> Oh — you're the one who came down the hill.
> Everyone said someone came down the hill.

*She waits. He doesn't say anything. She doesn't seem to notice.*

> Well. Welcome down, then.

**Second villager:**
> You'll want the well if you're thirsty. Middle of
> town, can't miss it. Rope's broken, back-when,
> but the water's fine.

**A child:**
> Are you staying? Everybody's leaving.
> Not in a bad way. Just — later-on, up in the
> spring, mostly.

**The shopkeeper:**
> Haven't seen you. That's alright, I don't see
> everyone.

*Asking about the kingdom:*

> Limpo? You're standing in it. Well — Okobo's in
> it, and it's in Limpo. Same thing from far
> enough off.

> It's been thin here a long while. Two hundred
> years of thin. You get used to which fields you
> don't bother with.

*Asking about Yettallia:*

> Over east. They do alright. They've been doing
> alright at us for a while now.

*She says this without any heat at all, and changes the subject.*

**The war memorial** *(no NPC stands near it):*
> A list of names, cut into stone.
> It is a long list for a village this size.

**Notes.** Nobody asks him a single question in this scene. Every villager is friendly, every
villager talks about themselves or the village, and not one of them says "what's your name?" — the
game already knows it, and nobody in Okobo ever needs it. The first character in the entire game to
use `[NAME]` unprompted is a Vixtry receptionist in Act 4.

## 4. Act 3 — Vixtry, three tiers

**Canvasser, Sable City transit hub** *(before combat):*

> Hi! Do you have a second to hear about
> co-living?

> It's not a game or anything. It's — you'd be
> living, just, in a nicer place. That's the
> whole pitch, honestly.

*After combat:*

> That's fine. That's totally fine.
> Have a good one!

**Field staff, campus lobby:**

> That's above my level. But I'd say — if it were
> a problem, we'd have heard.

> My dad's on the hardware side. He likes it.
> He says he likes it.

**Grunt, delivering the father reveal:**

*This is triggered by talking to a low-tier employee about nothing in particular, and it happens
mid-sentence in an otherwise unremarkable exchange.*

> Sorry, one sec — [NAME], right? Huh.
> Same last name as one of the hardware guys.

> Anyway. Second floor's closed today.

*He goes back to what he was doing. There is no music sting, no camera move, and no way to ask a
follow-up question.*

**Officer, campus, Act 4:**

> Dependency isn't the failure case. It's the
> retention curve working.

> You've been very consistent. Most subjects
> plateau around week six.

> I'm sorry. That isn't a figure of speech —
> I am sorry. It's still going to happen.

**Notes.** The officer apologizes and means it, and it changes nothing. That gap — sincere regret,
zero deviation — is the entire characterization of Vixtry Co., and every officer line should be
testable against it.

## 5. Act 5 — The Custodian, true form

*The Root. The arena narrows toward a point as phases advance. Grass spreads across the floor from
the edges inward.*

*Phases 1–3: he says his one line, in the degrading box, at each phase transition.*

> Never leave. Never leave. Never leave. Never
> leave. Never leave. Never leave. Never leave.
> Never leave. Never leave. Never leave. Never
> leave. Never leave. Never leave. Never leave.

*Entering the final phase, the box stops degrading. It renders clean, in the NPC font, for the only
time in the game:*

> I kept it warm.
> I kept it exactly the same.
> Nobody came looking.
> Never leave.

*No advance prompt. It sits there for six seconds and then the fight resumes.*

**Notes.** Four lines. It states what he wants and one true thing about why the boy might have
wanted it too. It explains nothing. Every draft that added a fifth line was worse, and the third
line is the only accusation in the game — and it isn't aimed at the player.

He does not speak again.

## 6. The Ending

*The bedroom. Same camera as Act 0. Same five interactables. Furnace, clock.*

*One thing is different. In the standard ending: the hall light is on, and the door is open a few
inches.*

**The window:**
> It's dark out. The neighbours' lights are off.

**The poster:**
> He's had it a long time.

**The dresser:**
> The second drawer sticks.

**The light switch:**
> The room light comes on.

**The door:**
> *(no text — the door is already open)*

*Walking through it ends the game.*

**Notes.** Four of the five descriptions are byte-identical to Act 0. The light switch is the
change, and it is one word of difference: *nothing happens* becomes *the room light comes on*.

Nothing in the scene comments on this. There is no epilogue text, no explanation of Vixtry, no
resolution about the father, and no one is waiting on the other side of the door. He can just open
it now.

*(Secret ending: two things are different. The second is his mother, sitting on the end of the bed,
asleep. She does not wake up, there is no dialogue, and she is not interactable.)*
